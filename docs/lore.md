# Hold the Line: the world and its history

The reference for the setting shared by *Hold the Line* and its planned real-time strategy
prequel. What players see lives in `scripts/data/campaign_data.gd` (the prologue, each people's
description, and every line of the story); this file holds the wider history the game only
touches on, and what the prequel can build on. Where the two differ, the game's text is canon.

Tone: solemn, mythic medieval fantasy. The theme, in both games: **no one holds the line alone.**

---

## The ages

| Era | When | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| The Age of Kingdoms | Before the Line | Many peoples, no single power. Trade and alliances, but also generations of war over mines, forests, farmland and roads. |
| The Founding | Centuries before the game | The peoples' delegates swear the oath at Dawnspire, the oath's birthplace and the alliance's heart. Stonehollow builds the beacons; the Mage Conclave enchants them, and first lights them from the Circle below the Arcane Spires. |
| The Wars of the Fading Line | After the Founding | Old rivalries return as open war. Members refuse to defend rivals, close roads, withhold supplies. **The prequel's era.** |
| The Breaking | About two hundred years before the game | Dawnspire's beacon goes dark first; the rest follow. The oaths are sealed in the Lost Archive. Dawnspire stands empty, its beacon cold, for some 150 years. |
| The Ogre King's conquest | About fifty years before the game | A chieftain of the wastes, then around thirty, having united the Monster Clans, walks into a Dawnspire no one defends and takes the Line's throne. For fifty years he spreads his reach a little further each year, while every people tells itself it's someone else's border. |
| *Hold the Line* | Now | The Commander at Ashwatch relights the beacons, region by region, and marches on Dawnspire. |

### The Line

A defensive alliance between independent peoples, never an empire or a single government.
Its members kept their own leaders, customs and interests, and swore two things: that when
one was threatened the others would answer, and that they would settle their disputes
without letting them endanger the continent.

> When one people is threatened, the others shall answer. No kingdom shall be left to stand alone.

**The beacons.** Ten fires: nine regional beacons and the tenth at Dawnspire, which stands for
the common oath rather than any one region's. Stonehollow's Engineers' Guild built the
towers; the Mage Conclave's enchantment lets a flame carry an unmistakable call across great
distances. The nine regional beacons, in campaign order: Ashwatch (the southern frontier),
Stonehollow, Greenwood, Mercy Vale, Deepdelve, Frostmarch, the High Monastery, the Free
Cities and the Arcane Spires. Dawnspire's can only be relit by every people together.

**Each beacon is lit its own way** (all established in the game): Ashwatch's by Tamsin on the
ridge; Stonehollow's by its masons on the cliff; Greenwood's on the great oak, from three
watchfires; Mercy Vale's from the shrine's lamp; Deepdelve's over the mine, once the forges
burn; Frostmarch's by the rite of three stones round the frozen lake (it burns blue);
the Monastery's by three bells rung at dawn; the Free Cities' over the harbour after a
unanimous Council vote; the Spires' fed by three spires.

### The Wars of the Fading Line and the Breaking

Nobody in particular broke the Line; everyone did, a little at a time, each for reasons
that seemed sound to them.

- Human rulers wanted farmland and trade corridors.
- Elven communities resisted the cutting of their forests.
- Dwarven guilds competed over mineral wealth.
- Beastfolk clans fought for migration routes that settled kingdoms kept cutting.
- Marshborn communities resisted the draining of wetlands and the turning of rivers.
- The Free Cities' councils put trade before their obligations; some profited from the wars.
- Even the orders that kept the beacons competed over the authority and resources to run them.
- The Monster Clans stayed divided: some traded, some raided, many were pushed back by expanding kingdoms.

When settlements were attacked, former allies stopped coming. Dawnspire's beacon went dark
first and the others followed as the roads fell out of use. The written oaths were sealed
in the Lost Archive beneath the Spires so no one need read what had been broken (*The Lost
Archive*: "every people broke it, for gold or land or pride").

### The Ogre King

- **Origin.** A powerful leader among the divided Monster Clans who saw that their
  division was their weakness. He defeated rival chieftains, made alliances, promised
  protection from the kingdoms. To some a unifier, to others already a tyrant. Unification
  became conquest.
- **His followers** believe in him, want revenge for old wars, want land and plunder, or
  obey from fear. Not every clan joined him; some resist him still, some fled.
- **Age.** Ogres live 90 to 120 years; he is around eighty in the game, and has held Dawnspire
  for fifty of them.
- **Dawnspire.** He did not cause the Breaking. He found a continent that could no longer
  defend itself and took a citadel nobody held ("They left it lying in the road. I only
  picked it up.").
- **His true power** came after, at Dawnspire: he drew on its dark beacon and the curse of
  every broken oath. The broken Line is the source of his strength; he inherited the
  betrayal and learned to use it. This is what unbinds him in the nine-beacon finale.
- **The Ash Marshal**, his herald, burns the road ahead of him and falls at Sable Crossing.

## The civilizations

The **eight peoples of the Line** in the game (the Engineers' Guild, the Woodland Watch, the
Sanctuary, the Mining Guild, the Northern Covenant, the Martial Order, the Free Cities, the
Mage Conclave) are *delegations*: regional, political, professional and cultural bodies,
several of them home to more than one race. They are not the races below. No race has a
beacon of its own, and none needs one: the Line the Commander rebuilds has room for every
people, beacon or not.

Six principal civilizations (the prequel's planned playable roster), and two rarer peoples.
No civilization is wholly good or wholly wicked, and no race shares one allegiance.

### Humans: the kingdoms of the heartlands
- **Lands:** the fertile plains: kingdoms, fortified towns, farms, trading cities.
- **Society:** politically divided; independent rulers and noble houses compete for influence.
- **War:** disciplined infantry, knights, archers, fortifications, coordinated armies; adaptable anywhere.
- **Old grievances:** territorial expansion, trade disputes, competition for strategic ground.
- **In the decline:** pushed for farmland and corridors into others' forests, rivers and plains.
- **In *Hold the Line*:** most of the resistance, Ashwatch above all. Captain Maren Hale and
  the Commander stand for an alliance larger than any one human kingdom.
- **Prequel role:** versatile, balanced armies, strong defences, a reliable economy.

### Elves: the woodland peoples
- **Lands:** ancient forests and secluded woodland settlements.
- **Society:** patience, tradition, craftsmanship, care for the land; long memories of old alliances and betrayals.
- **War:** archery, scouting, magical support, precision over numbers.
- **Old grievances:** encroachment on ancestral forests; distrust of fast-growing kingdoms.
- **Relationships:** some communities keep ties with the Fae, a distinct people.
- **In *Hold the Line*:** the Woodland Watch of Greenwood (green-haired archers; Warden Sylla
  Thorne, an elf, whose distrust of outsiders is her people's history). Thorne names the Fae
  as the groves' first keepers (*The Watchfires*).
- **Prequel role:** exceptional ranged units, mobile armies, woodland advantages, nature magic.

### Dwarves: the mountain holds
- **Lands:** fortified mountain settlements and great underground cities.
- **Society:** craft, engineering, mining, tradition. Not one polity: builders and engineers
  in some holds, miners and smiths in others, and fierce rivalries between them.
- **War:** strong defences, durable infantry, advanced siege engines.
- **Old grievances:** mineral deposits, trade agreements, mountain passes.
- **In *Hold the Line*:** Stonehollow's Engineers' Guild (dwarves for the most part; keepers of
  the beacon designs; Guildmaster Brannoc Vell) and Deepdelve's Mining Guild (dwarven miners
  and founders; Forewoman Dagna Coalbright). The guilds have argued over Deepdelve's gold
  since the old Line, and both still honour the oath (Dagna: "tell Vell the Mining Guild still
  remembers who paid for his towers. Then tell him thanks").
- **Prequel role:** defensive structures, tough infantry, siege equipment, efficient mining.

### The Monster Clans: the peoples of the wastes
- **Who:** ogres, goblins, trolls and others; an outsiders' name for many peoples, not one race or one nation.
- **Society:** settled and travelling; tribal councils, independent chieftains, warlords.
- **War:** shock troops, many cheap units, fast expansion, aggression.
- **Old grievances:** feuds among themselves, displacement, raiding and being raided by kingdoms.
- **In *Hold the Line*:** the Ogre King's horde is the largest coalition of clans, not all of
  them. Goblins who would not march for him reach the Sanctuary (*The Wounded Road*); ogres
  who would not bend the knee are among his prisoners at Dawnspire, and among those who
  light the tenth beacon.
- **Prequel role:** the rise of a powerful ogre who unites scattered clans.

### The Marshborn: the river and wetland peoples
- **Lands:** wetlands, river deltas, marshes, tropical lowlands; settlements built on and around the water.
- **Society:** an ancient reptilian people; ancestry, knowledge of the land, sacred waterways. Hunters, herbalists, scouts.
- **War:** spears, javelins, poison, ambush, mobility through ground others can't cross.
- **Old grievances:** drained wetlands, turned rivers, contested crossings, invasion.
- **In *Hold the Line*:** the King's armies took their rivers for his roads and supply lines.
  Marshborn herbalists now work in Mercy Vale and walk with the army's healers (*The Wounded
  Road*); Marshborn are among the prisoners at Dawnspire.
- **Prequel role:** terrain control, ambushes, poison, amphibious movement, specialised gathering.

### The Beastfolk: the clans of the open plains
- **Who:** centaurs, minotaurs and related clans; no single government.
- **Society:** nomadic or semi-nomadic; traditions tied to territories and migration routes. Some clans are old allies, some old rivals.
- **War:** speed, strength, aggressive tactics.
- **Old grievances:** lost territory, blocked routes, settled kingdoms against nomads.
- **In *Hold the Line*:** the King's expansion displaced many clans. Some serve him for promised
  land or protection; others refused. A master of the Martial Order is of the plains clans,
  "half her clan rides for the King, for land he promised them. The other half refused him,
  and lie on his road" (*The Thousand Steps*). Plains-clan folk trade in the Free Cities.
- **Prequel role:** fast armies, powerful melee, mobile settlements, raiding.

### The Fae: guardians of the ancient groves
- **Who:** dryads, treants, woodland spirits; magical beings of sacred and wild places. Not elves.
- **Society:** old traditions and obligations strange to mortal kingdoms; they keep out of
  politics unless their lands or ancient agreements are threatened.
- **Old grievances:** groves destroyed, natural magic misused, sacred boundaries crossed.
- **In *Hold the Line*:** present only in word: they kept Greenwood's groves "before the
  first elf walked under them", and Greenwood's fires burn by their leave.
- **Prequel role:** a neutral or later playable power (living structures, magical defences, terrain).

### The Dragonkin: keepers of the old flame
- **Who:** a reclusive draconic people in remote mountains and ancient strongholds; few in number.
- **Society:** keepers of powerful magic and forgotten histories; seldom intervene.
- **Old grievances:** declining strongholds, isolation, disputes over dangerous knowledge.
- **In *Hold the Line*:** their records lie in the Spires. In *The Lost Archive* Ysolde quotes
  them: "an oath sworn with magic does not end when it is broken. Its power goes somewhere."
  This deepens the mystery of the dark beacon without replacing it.
- **Prequel role:** a neutral or later elite civilization; rare, powerful individuals.

**Characters' races.** Established by art or text: Thorne is an elf (her portrait's ears;
the Watch's green hair). Vell and Dagna are dwarves (text; their portraits, a bald, bearded
guildmaster and a miner, agree). Everyone else is left open: Hale, Tamsin, Ilse, Ysra,
Kaito, Coralie, Ysolde and the Commander have no race fixed by their portraits or lines. The
Ogre King is an ogre; the Ash Marshal's people aren't stated.

## Magic

Magic belongs to no single race. Elves practise woodland and restorative magic; dwarven
craftsfolk work runes into what they make; Marshborn shamans and Beastfolk spirit-keepers
keep their own rites; the Fae are magical by nature; the Dragonkin keep the oldest
traditions. The Mage Conclave gathers and studies many of them.

**Necromancy** is a discipline, not a people: magic that moves the dead. Some think it a
dangerous but useful branch; others think it crosses a line no one should. The Conclave
keeps its techniques and argues over their use. This is the Mage's Necromancy path, and how
the Ogre King fields skeletons and Skeleton Archers: necromancers who serve him willingly,
under threat, or whose knowledge he took. There is no undead faction. His *Grave Rite*
(unbound) is different in scale and source: the Spires' broken oath raising the dead
round him.

**The arts of the broken oaths** (the Ogre King, unbound; one per people, from the oath it
broke): Oathbreaker's Volley (Greenwood), Deep Charges (Deepdelve), Iron Bolt
(Stonehollow), Grave Rite (the Spires), Oath of Winter (Frostmarch), Spirit Bomb (the
Monastery), Discordant Dirge (the Free Cities), Stolen Mercy (Mercy Vale).

## For the prequel

- **Setting:** the Wars of the Fading Line, as the alliance strains and fails.
- **Its loop has a reason:** kingdoms expand because their lands can't carry their
  ambitions; every new mine, forest, field or pass promises security and risks a war.
- **Playable:** Humans, Elves, Dwarves, Monster Clans, Marshborn, Beastfolk. The Fae and
  Dragonkin as neutral or rare powers first.
- **Perspectives may disagree:** a heroic defence to one people is an invasion to another.
- **Arcs to explore:** human border wars and shaky alliances; elves choosing between
  isolation and cooperation; dwarven mining and engineering rivalries; Marshborn holding
  their waterways; Beastfolk defending clan freedom; an ogre uniting the clans.
- **The link:** the prequel shows how the peoples abandoned the alliance; *Hold the Line*
  shows their descendants paying for it and rebuilding it.
- **Not yet canon:** specific prequel missions, leaders, battles or technologies.

## Settled questions

1. **When:** the Line was sworn **centuries ago**; its beacons have been dark for **two hundred
   years** (since the Breaking). In the game: Ysolde, "sworn at Dawnspire, centuries ago";
   Vell, "Centuries, and the old work still stands"; every "two hundred years dark" stays.
2. **The Ogre King's reign:** about eighty years old (ogres live 90 to 120), he has held
   Dawnspire for **fifty years**, after it had stood empty for some 150. In the game:
   "Dawnspire has been mine for fifty years" (seven-or-eight-beacon route); Ysolde at the
   Spires, "For fifty years he has sat in Dawnspire, reaching a little further every year";
   the standard ending, "Fifty years under him, two hundred years dark".
3. **Where:** **Dawnspire is the birthplace of the oath**; the Line sat and swore on its throne
   (as the Ogre King says). The Circle below the Spires is where the Conclave first lit the
   beacons. In the game: Ysolde, "The Line was sworn at Dawnspire... Its beacons were first
   lit from this Circle."
4. **The Engineer's look:** redrawn as a dwarf (short and broad, a great ginger beard over a
   leather apron, a leather cap with brass goggles, a tool belt, the hammer on his shoulder).
